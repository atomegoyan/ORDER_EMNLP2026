\section{Dataset: HistoriQA-ThirdRepublic}
\label{sec:dataset}

We build on HistoriQA-ThirdRepublic~\cite{historiqa}, a French
historian-validated benchmark over three heterogeneous 1887 sources:
the parliamentary transcripts of the Chambre des D\'eput\'es
(\textit{Les D\'ebats}) and two ideologically opposed dailies
(\textit{Le Gaulois}, \textit{L'Intransigeant}). The benchmark
contains  885 are multi-hop, split into
571 newspaper\,$\to$\,\textit{D\'ebats} pairs and 314 cross-newspaper
pairs. Full corpus statistics, historical context, and question-type
breakdown are in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix-dataset}
(Table~\ref{tab:corpus-stats}).

\paragraph{Retrieval challenges that shape our method.}
Three structural properties drive the framework: (i)~\emph{length
heterogeneity} --- \textit{D\'ebats} documents are an order of
magnitude longer than newspaper articles, ruling out a single chunking
granularity and motivating per-cluster chunking
(Section~\ref{sec:indexing-results}); (ii)~\emph{corpus imbalance} ---
\textit{Les D\'ebats} is more than $6\times$ larger than the two
newspapers combined and supplies 80.6\% of Naive-baseline top-3 hits
even on newspaper-only gold~\cite{historiqa}; and (iii)~\emph{cross-source
multi-hop reasoning} --- the multi-hop questions above require
evidence from multiple corpora, a structure that top-$k$ cosine
ranking cannot guarantee since the dominant corpus may consume all
slots. Properties (ii) and (iii) motivate the source-routing (QRe)
and uniform multi-source (UMS) strategies of
Section~\ref{sec:retrieval-results}.
